## Chapter 4: Privacy, Safety, and Societal Impacts of AI — Flashcards

| # | Front (Question) | Back (Answer) |
|---|-----------------|---------------|
| 1 | Differential privacy epsilon=0.1 vs epsilon=5.0 - which provides stronger privacy protection? | Epsilon=0.1 provides MUCH stronger privacy. Lower epsilon = stronger privacy, noisier results. Epsilon=0.1 is strong privacy, epsilon=5.0 is weak privacy with high utility. Exam tip: Remember inverse relationship - higher number = weaker privacy. |
| 2 | Federated learning vs differential privacy - when to use which for privacy protection? | Federated learning: when data CANNOT be centralized (data locality requirements, distributed training). Differential privacy: when publishing aggregate STATISTICS or query results from centralized data. Exam tip: Federated = distributed training, Differential = safe statistics release. |
| 3 | What is disparate impact in employment AI systems? | Discriminatory outcomes that disproportionately affect protected groups EVEN WITHOUT using protected characteristics as inputs. Example: 75% approval for one group vs 40% for another. Illegal under Title VII unless business necessity proven. Exam tip: Can occur through proxy variables like zip code. |
| 4 | Model poisoning attack vs adversarial example attack - key difference? | Model poisoning: corrupts TRAINING data/process to compromise the model during training. Adversarial examples: crafted inputs that fool an ALREADY-TRAINED model at inference time. Exam tip: Poisoning = training-time, Adversarial = test-time. |
| 5 | What US law governs AI-based lending discrimination and what does it prohibit? | Equal Credit Opportunity Act (ECOA) and Regulation B. Prohibits credit discrimination based on protected characteristics AND disparate impact - even when protected characteristics aren't direct inputs. Exam tip: ECOA applies to disparate impact, not just explicit discrimination. |
| 6 | Clinical decision support system vs diagnostic AI - liability difference? | Clinical decision support (CDS): physician retains final authority and primary liability; AI assists. Diagnostic AI: makes autonomous decisions, different liability model (may involve vendor). Exam tip: CDS liability stays with licensed professional making final call. |
| 7 | Synthetic data generation for privacy - main advantage and main limitation? | Advantage: No real personal data used, eliminates direct privacy risk. Limitation: Loses fidelity for edge cases, rare conditions, and complex patterns - may not capture real-world distribution accurately. Exam tip: Great for privacy, poor for rare events critical in healthcare/safety. |
| 8 | Four-Fifths Rule in employment discrimination - what is it? | EEOC guideline: selection rate for protected group should be at least 80% (4/5) of the rate for the highest group. Example: if 50% of one group hired, other groups should be ≥40%. Below this triggers disparate impact investigation. Exam tip: 80% threshold is key number. |
| 9 | Adversarial robustness testing - what attack method finds minimal perturbations to fool a model? | Gradient-based optimization methods like FGSM (Fast Gradient Sign Method) or PGD (Projected Gradient Descent). These compute gradients to find minimal input changes that maximize loss/misclassification. Exam tip: Gradient-based = finds minimal adversarial perturbations efficiently. |
| 10 | Federated learning - what is transmitted between edge devices and central server? | Only MODEL UPDATES (weights, gradients) are transmitted - NOT raw data. Training happens locally on edge devices. Central server aggregates updates to improve global model. Exam tip: Raw data never leaves edge devices in federated learning. |
| 11 | AI employment monitoring leading to automated termination - primary governance requirement? | Meaningful HUMAN OVERSIGHT for high-stakes employment decisions. Automated systems can flag/recommend, but humans must review before termination. Exam tip: High-stakes decisions (employment, lending, healthcare) require human-in-the-loop. |
| 12 | When is differential privacy epsilon considered 'strong privacy' vs 'weak privacy'? | Strong privacy: epsilon < 1.0 (typically 0.1-0.5). Moderate: epsilon 1.0-3.0. Weak privacy: epsilon > 3.0 (e.g., 5.0+). Exam tip: Lower epsilon = more noise added = stronger privacy guarantee but less accurate results. |
