## Chapter 2: AI Development Lifecycle and Documentation — Flashcards

| # | Front (Question) | Back (Answer) |
|---|-----------------|---------------|
| 1 | EU AI Act Article 27: What must providers of high-risk AI systems complete before deployment? | Fundamental Rights Impact Assessment (FRIA). Must assess potential impact on fundamental rights including health, privacy, non-discrimination. Mandatory for high-risk AI (medical, employment, law enforcement, critical infrastructure). Exam tip: FRIA is legal requirement under Article 27, not optional like model cards. |
| 2 | AI lifecycle stage where legal/regulatory feasibility and use case appropriateness must be established? | Problem Definition (first stage). Must determine: Is AI appropriate? Is it legally permissible (high-risk classification)? What are success criteria? Sets constraints for all subsequent stages. Exam tip: Legal compliance assessed at problem definition, not after model development. |
| 3 | Model card (Mitchell et al. 2019): What are the 3 mandatory sections? | 1) Model details (version, type, intended use), 2) Performance metrics (disaggregated by demographic groups), 3) Limitations and bias considerations. Exam tip: Disaggregated metrics are critical — must show performance across protected characteristics to reveal disparate impact. |
| 4 | Datasheet for datasets (Gebru et al. 2018): Primary purpose in AI governance? | Document dataset creation motivation, composition, collection process, preprocessing, distribution, and maintenance. Enables assessment of data representativeness and potential biases. Exam tip: Datasheets address data provenance; model cards address model performance. Different artifacts. |
| 5 | Concept drift vs. Data drift: Which requires model retraining? | Concept drift requires retraining. Concept drift = relationship between features and target changes (e.g., what predicts creditworthiness shifts). Data drift = input distribution changes but relationship stays same (may not need retraining, just monitoring). Exam tip: Concept drift → retrain. Data drift → monitor and possibly retrain. |
| 6 | Third-party AI model procurement: Strongest contractual liability protection clause? | Indemnification clause where vendor assumes liability for AI outputs that cause harm or violate laws (discrimination, defamation, IP infringement). Transfers legal and financial risk to vendor. Exam tip: SLAs cover uptime, not output liability. Indemnification protects against harmful outputs. |
| 7 | Post-deployment continuous monitoring: Who has PRIMARY accountability for acting on performance degradation? | Business unit deploying the model (system owner). They own outcomes and must act on monitoring insights. Data scientists = builders, MLOps = infrastructure maintainers, Governance = oversight. Exam tip: Accountability shifts from builders to business owners at deployment stage. |
| 8 | EU AI Act Annex III: Name 3 use cases classified as high-risk AI requiring conformity assessment. | 1) Biometric identification/categorization (facial recognition), 2) Employment decisions (recruiting, promotion, termination), 3) Access to essential services (credit scoring, insurance). Also: education, law enforcement, critical infrastructure. Exam tip: High-risk = strict requirements including FRIA, third-party assessment, CE marking. |
| 9 | Data quality requirement: What does 'representativeness' mean for training data? | Training data must reflect the distribution and diversity of the real-world deployment population across relevant characteristics (demographics, geography, use contexts). Prevents systematic bias against underrepresented groups. Exam tip: 16-point accuracy drop across regions = non-representative data → return to data collection stage. |
| 10 | Model version control: When is a version change mandatory vs. optional? | Mandatory version change: Model retraining, architecture changes, feature engineering changes, anything affecting predictions. Optional: Infrastructure changes (API endpoint), monitoring additions that don't alter model. Exam tip: If predictions could differ, new version required for auditability and rollback capability. |
| 11 | Vendor due diligence for third-party models: Minimum required information for procurement decision? | 1) Training data sources and representativeness, 2) Performance metrics disaggregated by demographics, 3) Known limitations and failure modes, 4) Update/versioning policy, 5) Liability allocation in contract. Exam tip: Cannot assess bias risk without knowing training data composition — this is procurement blocker. |
| 12 | AI lifecycle stages in order (6 stages)? | 1) Problem Definition, 2) Data Collection/Preparation, 3) Model Development, 4) Validation, 5) Deployment, 6) Monitoring. Lifecycle is iterative — monitoring may trigger return to earlier stages. Exam tip: Validation comes BEFORE deployment; problem definition establishes legal/regulatory constraints first. |
