## Chapter 6: ISO/IEC 42001 and AI Governance Programs — Flashcards

| # | Front (Question) | Back (Answer) |
|---|-----------------|---------------|
| 1 | ISO/IEC 42001 Clause 4 two mandatory steps? | 1) Understand organization context and 2) Determine interested parties' needs/expectations related to AI management system. Exam tip: Context comes before policy (Clause 5) or planning (Clause 6) — it's foundational. |
| 2 | Five mandatory fields in AI system inventory per ISO/IEC 42001? | System ID, use case, risk tier, data sources, accountability assignment. Exam tip: 'Owner' alone isn't enough — must specify who's accountable for decisions and outcomes. |
| 3 | ISO/IEC 42001 Clause 5 policy must include which commitment? | Continual improvement of the AI management system (mandatory). Also should include: compliance with requirements, framework for objectives. Exam tip: Continual improvement is non-negotiable for certification. |
| 4 | Difference between third-party audit, self-assessment, and notified body review? | Third-party audit: accredited body certifies ISO/IEC 42001 compliance. Self-assessment: internal evaluation, no certification. Notified body: EU AI Act-specific, required for high-risk systems. Exam tip: High-risk AI in EU needs notified body, but can leverage ISO/IEC 42001 cert for governance portions. |
| 5 | What comes first: AI policy (Clause 5) or risk assessment (Clause 6)? | AI policy (Clause 5) comes first. Structure: Clause 4 context → Clause 5 leadership/policy → Clause 6 planning/risk. Exam tip: You can't plan without policy direction, but policy needs context understanding. |
| 6 | AI-specific risks vs. general enterprise risks — what's the key difference? | AI-specific: algorithmic bias, fairness, explainability, data quality impacts on model behavior, automated decision-making harms. General: financial, operational, cybersecurity, compliance. Exam tip: If question asks about AI risk assessment, look for fairness/bias/transparency answers, not just security/cost. |
| 7 | ISO/IEC 42001 Annex A controls — what are they? | Catalog of AI-specific controls covering AI lifecycle: data governance, model development, deployment, monitoring, impact assessment, human oversight. Exam tip: Annex A controls are selected based on risk assessment in Clause 6, applied in Clause 8 operations. |
| 8 | Can low-risk AI systems skip inventory documentation? | NO. All AI systems must be in inventory with core fields (ID, use case, risk tier, data sources, accountability). Proportionality applies to *controls*, not inventory. Exam tip: Inventory is comprehensive; control depth varies by risk. |
| 9 | ISO/IEC 42001 Clause 9 performance evaluation requires what three activities? | 1) Monitoring and measurement, 2) Internal audit, 3) Management review. Exam tip: Look for evidence of ongoing activities (dashboards, review minutes, audit reports), not one-time setup docs. |
| 10 | What is 'presumption of conformity' for EU AI Act? | If AI system conforms to harmonized standard (like ISO/IEC 42001), it's presumed to meet corresponding EU AI Act requirements. Exam tip: ISO/IEC 42001 helps with governance requirements but doesn't eliminate need for notified body on high-risk systems. |
| 11 | ISO/IEC 42001 published year and significance? | 2023 — first certifiable AI management system standard. Exam tip: Unlike guidance documents (NIST AI RMF), ISO/IEC 42001 enables third-party certification and legal compliance demonstration. |
| 12 | Proportionate governance in ISO/IEC 42001 — what does it mean? | Apply controls based on AI system risk level. High-risk = comprehensive controls/documentation. Low-risk = lighter controls but still in inventory with accountability. Exam tip: Proportionality applies to control implementation, not whether system is governed at all. |
