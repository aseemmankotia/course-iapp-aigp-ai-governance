## Chapter 3: Responsible AI: Fairness, Bias, and Transparency — Flashcards

| # | Front (Question) | Back (Answer) |
|---|-----------------|---------------|
| 1 | Four types of bias the AIGP exam tests: Historical, Representation, Measurement, Aggregation—define Measurement bias | Measurement bias: Using proxy variables that don't accurately measure the target. Example: zip code proxies for socioeconomic status but actually encodes race due to segregation. Exam tip: Measurement bias questions often involve 'indirect discrimination' via proxies. |
| 2 | Demographic parity vs Equalized odds—which requires equal FALSE POSITIVE rates across groups? | Equalized odds requires equal TPR AND equal FPR across groups. Demographic parity only requires equal positive prediction rates (doesn't care about error rates). Exam tip: Equalized odds is stricter—tests both accuracy metrics, not just outcomes. |
| 3 | Pre-processing vs In-processing vs Post-processing bias mitigation—when is each applied in ML pipeline? | Pre-processing: modify training data before training (reweighting, resampling). In-processing: modify algorithm during training (fairness constraints in loss function). Post-processing: adjust predictions after training (threshold calibration). Exam tip: In-processing is during training, not after. |
| 4 | SHAP vs LIME—which provides global explanations and which provides local? | LIME: LOCAL only (explains individual predictions via local linear approximation). SHAP: both LOCAL (Shapley values per prediction) and GLOBAL (aggregate SHAP values). Exam tip: 'LIME is Local' mnemonic. SHAP more versatile. |
| 5 | GDPR Article 22 'right to explanation'—what does it actually require? | Article 22 grants right NOT to be subject to solely automated decisions with legal/significant effects—requires HUMAN INTERVENTION, not explanation. Articles 13-15 require explanation of logic. Exam tip: Article 22 = human review requirement, NOT explanation requirement. |
| 6 | EU AI Act Article 14 human oversight for high-risk systems—which model: human-in-loop, on-loop, or in-command? | Human-in-the-loop required for high-risk systems: humans review and approve EACH AI decision before action. Human-on-the-loop (monitoring) and human-in-command (system control) insufficient for high-risk. Exam tip: High-risk = individual decision review, not aggregate monitoring. |
| 7 | Differential Privacy epsilon (ε) parameter—does lower ε = stronger or weaker privacy? | Lower ε = STRONGER privacy (less distinguishability, more noise, lower utility). ε=0.1 is strong privacy. ε=10 is weak privacy. Exam tip: ε is privacy budget—smaller budget = less spending = more protection. |
| 8 | Federated Learning—what is shared between participants: raw data, model updates, or synthetic data? | Model updates (gradients/weights) are shared, NOT raw data. Each participant trains locally on private data, sends only updates to central server. Exam tip: Federated learning = decentralized training, centralized aggregation of parameters only. |
| 9 | Counterfactual explanation vs SHAP value—what's the difference in what they provide? | Counterfactual: 'change X to Y to get different outcome' (actionable changes). SHAP: 'feature X contributed Z to this prediction' (attribution). Exam tip: Counterfactuals answer 'how to change outcome', SHAP answers 'why this outcome'. |
| 10 | Individual fairness vs Group fairness (demographic parity, equalized odds)—which is easier to measure? | Group fairness metrics (demographic parity, equalized odds) are easier to measure—compare statistics across defined groups. Individual fairness ('similar people treated similarly') requires defining similarity metric, much harder to operationalize. Exam tip: Group fairness = practical, individual fairness = theoretical ideal. |
| 11 | Saliency maps—what type of model explanation technique and what does it show? | Visual explanation for image/text models—highlights input regions most important to prediction (e.g., which pixels in image influenced classification). Local explanation method. Exam tip: Saliency maps are for visual/text data, not tabular data. |
| 12 | Synthetic data for privacy—what's the key risk that exam tests? | Synthetic data can leak information about training data if generator memorizes examples (membership inference attacks). Must validate: (1) privacy preservation (2) statistical fidelity (3) utility for downstream tasks. Exam tip: Synthetic ≠ automatically private, especially with GANs on small datasets. |
