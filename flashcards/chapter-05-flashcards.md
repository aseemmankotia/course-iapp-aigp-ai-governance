## Chapter 5: NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF) — Flashcards

| # | Front (Question) | Back (Answer) |
|---|-----------------|---------------|
| 1 | NIST AI RMF: Four core functions and their relationship | GOVERN (overarching, continuous), MAP (context/categorize risks), MEASURE (metrics/benchmarks), MANAGE (respond/improve). GOVERN enables all others; MAP→MEASURE→MANAGE is sequential but iterative. Exam tip: GOVERN is NOT just first step—it runs throughout lifecycle |
| 2 | NIST AI RMF: Seven trustworthy characteristics (list all) | 1) Valid/Reliable 2) Safe 3) Secure/Resilient 4) Accountable/Transparent 5) Explainable/Interpretable 6) Privacy-Enhanced 7) Fair with Harmful Bias Managed. Exam tip: All must be measured in MEASURE function, not just high-risk ones |
| 3 | Explainability vs Interpretability in NIST AI RMF | Interpretability = technical understanding of HOW model makes decisions (internal logic). Explainability = ability to COMMUNICATE decisions to stakeholders. Exam tip: Heavily tested distinction—interpretability is technical, explainability is communication |
| 4 | NIST AI RMF legal status and purpose | Voluntary framework, NOT regulation. Designed for risk management and to enable crosswalks to other frameworks (EU AI Act, ISO/IEC 42001, OECD). Widely adopted for compliance mapping. Exam tip: 'Voluntary' is tested—it's not mandatory but commonly used |
| 5 | GOVERN function: Key components | Policies, roles/responsibilities, organizational culture, oversight structures, accountability mechanisms. Runs continuously, not just at start. Exam tip: GOVERN is overarching function that enables MAP/MEASURE/MANAGE |
| 6 | MAP function: Three main activities | 1) Context establishment (intended purpose, stakeholders, deployment environment) 2) Risk categorization across trustworthy characteristics 3) Risk mapping across AI lifecycle stages. Exam tip: Categorization means ALL trustworthy characteristics, not just obvious ones |
| 7 | MEASURE function: What must be measured | Metrics and benchmarks for ALL seven trustworthy characteristics, with emphasis based on MAP findings. Includes technical performance AND trustworthy characteristic metrics. Exam tip: Not just model accuracy—must measure fairness, privacy, explainability, etc. |
| 8 | MANAGE function: Three core activities | 1) Risk response (treat/accept/transfer/avoid) 2) Continuous improvement (monitor, update) 3) Incident response (document, assess, respond, communicate). Exam tip: 'Manage' ≠ 'eliminate'—bias/risks are managed, not necessarily eliminated |
| 9 | NIST AI RMF vs EU AI Act: Risk tiering difference | EU AI Act: Fixed categories by use case (unacceptable/high/limited/minimal). NIST AI RMF: Context-dependent assessment across trustworthy characteristics, NO preset tiers. Exam tip: Heavily tested—NIST has no predefined risk levels like EU does |
| 10 | NIST AI RMF Playbook: Purpose and usage | Provides SUGGESTED actions and implementation guidance for each function. Not prescriptive—must be customized to organizational context. Includes examples, not requirements. Exam tip: Playbook = suggestions, not mandatory steps |
| 11 | Crosswalks in NIST AI RMF: Available frameworks | ISO/IEC 42001, EU AI Act, OECD AI Principles, others. Allows mapping NIST controls to other framework requirements without duplicate governance. Exam tip: Crosswalk feature enables multi-framework compliance from single NIST implementation |
| 12 | Fair with Harmful Bias Managed: What this means | 'Managed' not 'eliminated'—recognizes bias is ongoing risk requiring continuous mitigation, monitoring, documentation. Doesn't require zero bias or immediate system shutdown. Exam tip: Common trap—'managed' ≠ 'eliminated' or 'prevented' |
