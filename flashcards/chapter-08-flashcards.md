## Chapter 8: EU AI Act Part 1: Risk Tiers and Prohibited Practices — Flashcards

| # | Front (Question) | Back (Answer) |
|---|-----------------|---------------|
| 1 | EU AI Act: Four risk tiers in order from highest to lowest regulatory burden | 1) Unacceptable (prohibited), 2) High-risk, 3) Limited-risk (transparency), 4) Minimal-risk. Exam tip: Most questions test prohibited vs high-risk distinction and corresponding obligations. |
| 2 | EU AI Act Article 5: Effective date for prohibited AI practices | February 2, 2025. Exam tip: This is earlier than high-risk AI enforcement (August 2, 2026). Prohibited practices are banned immediately upon this date. |
| 3 | EU AI Act: Maximum penalty for prohibited AI violations | €35 million or 7% of global annual turnover, whichever is higher. Exam tip: This is the highest penalty tier. High-risk non-compliance is €15M or 3%. |
| 4 | EU AI Act Article 5: Five main categories of prohibited AI practices | 1) Social scoring by public authorities, 2) Manipulation/subliminal techniques causing harm, 3) Exploitation of vulnerable groups, 4) Biometric categorization (race, politics, religion), 5) Real-time remote biometric in public (with law enforcement exceptions). Exam tip: Know all five categories. |
| 5 | EU AI Act: Enforcement date for most high-risk AI systems in Annex III | August 2, 2026. Exam tip: This is later than prohibited AI (Feb 2, 2025). Most Annex III categories enforce on this date, though some have different timelines. |
| 6 | EU AI Act: Where is emotion recognition AI prohibited? | Workplace and education settings (Article 5). NOT prohibited in healthcare or other contexts. Exam tip: Common trap is thinking it's banned everywhere—it's only workplace/education. |
| 7 | EU AI Act Annex III: Seven high-risk AI categories | 1) Biometric identification, 2) Critical infrastructure, 3) Education/employment, 4) Law enforcement, 5) Migration/asylum, 6) Justice/democratic processes, 7) Essential services. Exam tip: Education admission AI and employment recruitment AI are high-risk. |
| 8 | EU AI Act Article 5: Real-time remote biometric identification (RBI) in public—prohibited or allowed? | Generally prohibited BUT has law enforcement exceptions: search for missing children, prevention of terrorist threats, detection of serious crimes. Requires judicial authorization. Exam tip: Not absolute prohibition. |
| 9 | EU AI Act: High-risk AI mandatory requirements (name at least 6) | 1) Risk management, 2) Data governance, 3) Technical documentation, 4) Record-keeping, 5) Transparency, 6) Human oversight, 7) Accuracy/robustness, 8) Cybersecurity. Exam tip: All eight are testable—know what each means. |
| 10 | EU AI Act Article 50: Limited-risk transparency obligations | 1) Chatbot disclosure (users must know they're interacting with AI), 2) Deepfake labeling (AI-generated content disclosure). Exam tip: Simple customer service chatbots require disclosure even if not high-risk. |
| 11 | EU AI Act: General-purpose AI (GPAI) obligations—do they vary? | Yes, obligations vary by systemic risk (Articles 52-53). Models with systemic risk have additional requirements beyond standard GPAI obligations. Exam tip: Not all foundation models have same requirements. |
| 12 | EU AI Act: Is social scoring by private companies prohibited? | No. Article 5 prohibits social scoring by PUBLIC AUTHORITIES only. Private social scoring may be high-risk or limited-risk depending on context. Exam tip: Common trap—prohibition is public sector only. |
