## Chapter 9: EU AI Act Part 2: High-Risk Compliance & Global AI Laws — Flashcards

| # | Front (Question) | Back (Answer) |
|---|-----------------|---------------|
| 1 | EU AI Act Article 9: What must high-risk AI risk management systems be? | Continuous and iterative process throughout AI lifecycle, documented, systematic, proportionate. Must identify foreseeable risks, estimate/evaluate risks, adopt mitigation measures, test system. Exam tip: 'Continuous' and 'iterative' are key—one-time risk assessment isn't compliant. |
| 2 | EU AI Act conformity assessment: Annex VI vs Annex VII difference? | Annex VI = internal assessment (most high-risk AI), provider self-assesses and issues CE marking. Annex VII = third-party notified body required for biometric identification and critical infrastructure. Exam trap: assuming all high-risk AI needs notified body—only specific categories do. |
| 3 | GDPR Article 22 and EU AI Act intersection: what's required for automated decisions? | Need: 1) Human oversight (AI Act Article 14), 2) Transparency to deployer (Article 13), 3) Meaningful information about logic to data subject (GDPR Article 22). Exam tip: All three required together—partial compliance fails. |
| 4 | EU AI Act Article 10 data governance: what bias-related requirement exists? | Must examine training/validation/testing datasets for biases, identify data gaps, ensure datasets are relevant, representative, free of errors, complete. Exam note: 'Examine for biases' is mandatory action, not optional best practice. |
| 5 | EU AI Act Article 12: what must logs for high-risk AI enable? | Logs must enable traceability of system functioning throughout its lifecycle. Automatically generated, stored in appropriate/secure manner. Retention: appropriate period for purpose. Exam trap: no fixed retention period—must be context-appropriate. |
| 6 | Colorado AI Act (2026): what triggers impact assessment requirement? | High-risk AI systems making consequential decisions (employment, education, financial services, housing, insurance, legal services, healthcare). Must assess: purpose, benefits, known harms, transparency, data governance. Exam note: employment AI is explicitly covered. |
| 7 | US Executive Order 14110 (October 2023): key AI safety requirement? | Developers of foundation models posing national security/safety risks must share safety test results with US government before public release. Threshold: models trained using >10^26 integer/floating-point operations. Exam tip: specific compute threshold = 10^26 ops. |
| 8 | New York City Local Law 144: what must employers do before using AI hiring tools? | Conduct annual bias audit, publish audit results, notify candidates about AI use. Applies to automated employment decision tools (AEDT). Exam note: annual frequency is required, not one-time audit. |
| 9 | GDPR DPIA (Article 35): when is prior consultation with supervisory authority required? | When DPIA indicates high risk that cannot be mitigated by controller. Article 36 prior consultation only if residual high risk remains after planned mitigation. Exam trap: not all DPIAs require authority consultation—only unmitigated high-risk cases. |
| 10 | EU AI Act Article 13: transparency obligations to deployers—what info required? | Must provide: intended purpose, level of accuracy/robustness, known limitations, foreseeable misuse. Info must enable deployer to comply with obligations. Exam note: transparency to deployer (Article 13) ≠ transparency to end-users (different requirement). |
| 11 | EU AI Act Article 15: accuracy, robustness, cybersecurity—what's required? | Achieve appropriate level of accuracy/robustness/cybersecurity throughout lifecycle. Be resilient against errors, faults, inconsistencies, third-party attempts to alter performance. Exam tip: 'appropriate level' means context-dependent, not absolute standards. |
| 12 | GDPR legitimate interest (Article 6(1)(f)) for AI training: what test must be passed? | Three-part test: 1) legitimate interest exists, 2) processing necessary for that interest, 3) balance test—interest doesn't override individual's rights/freedoms. Must document legitimate interest assessment (LIA). Exam trap: all three parts required, not just business need. |
