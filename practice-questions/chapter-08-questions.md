## Chapter 8: EU AI Act Part 1: Risk Tiers and Prohibited Practices — Practice Questions

**Q1.** A city government in France wants to implement an AI system that assigns social scores to citizens based on their behavior, which determines access to public services. The system will launch in March 2025. What is the regulatory status of this system under the EU AI Act?

A) Allowed as a high-risk AI system if proper conformity assessment is completed by August 2026
B) Prohibited effective February 2, 2025 under Article 5 as an unacceptable risk practice
C) Permitted with transparency obligations as a limited-risk AI system
D) Allowed if human oversight mechanisms are implemented and documented

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: B**

Article 5 of the EU AI Act explicitly prohibits social scoring by public authorities. This prohibition is effective February 2, 2025, making such systems illegal regardless of safeguards implemented.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- Social scoring is categorically prohibited, not classified as high-risk. No conformity assessment can make it compliant.
- Transparency obligations apply to limited-risk AI like chatbots. Social scoring is prohibited entirely, not subject to transparency requirements.
- Prohibited AI practices cannot be made compliant through technical measures like human oversight. The practice itself is banned.

**Q2.** A European university deploys an AI system in September 2025 to evaluate student applications and determine admissions decisions. Under the EU AI Act, what is the compliance timeline for this system?

A) Immediately compliant as education AI is minimal-risk
B) Must achieve compliance by February 2, 2025 when the Act takes effect
C) Must achieve compliance by August 2, 2026 as a high-risk AI system in Annex III
D) Must achieve compliance by 2027 as education systems have extended timelines

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: C**

AI systems used for admission or evaluation in educational institutions are classified as high-risk under Annex III. Most high-risk AI categories must comply by August 2, 2026.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- Education and training AI for admission/evaluation purposes is explicitly high-risk under Annex III, not minimal-risk.
- February 2, 2025 is the effective date for prohibited AI practices only, not high-risk system requirements.
- August 2, 2026 is the standard enforcement date for most Annex III high-risk categories including education. No 2027 extension exists for education systems.

</details>

**Q3.** A retail company deploys an AI chatbot on its website that provides customer service without disclosing it is AI. The chatbot does not collect biometric data or make high-risk decisions. What EU AI Act obligation applies?

A) High-risk AI conformity assessment under Annex III
B) Transparency obligation under Article 50 to disclose AI interaction
C) No obligations as customer service is minimal-risk with no regulatory requirements
D) Prohibited AI practices compliance under Article 5

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: B**

Article 50 requires limited-risk AI systems, including chatbots that interact with humans, to disclose that the user is interacting with an AI system. This is a transparency obligation.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- Customer service chatbots without high-risk functions are not classified as high-risk AI requiring conformity assessment.
- While the underlying risk may be minimal, the EU AI Act specifically imposes transparency obligations on systems that interact with natural persons, making disclosure mandatory.
- Article 5 covers prohibited practices like manipulation and social scoring, not customer service chatbots.

**Q4.** A company operating across the EU develops an AI system that manipulates human behavior using subliminal techniques beyond a person's consciousness, causing them physical harm. What is the maximum penalty under the EU AI Act?

A) €15 million or 3% of global annual turnover, whichever is higher
B) €7 million or 1.5% of global annual turnover, whichever is higher
C) €35 million or 7% of global annual turnover, whichever is higher
D) €50 million or 10% of global annual turnover, whichever is higher

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: C**

Subliminal manipulation causing harm is a prohibited AI practice under Article 5. Violations of prohibited AI practices carry the highest penalty: up to €35 million or 7% of global annual turnover, whichever is higher.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- €15 million or 3% is the penalty for high-risk AI non-compliance, not prohibited practices.
- €7 million or 1.5% is not a penalty tier in the EU AI Act.
- €50 million or 10% exceeds the maximum penalties established in the EU AI Act.

</details>

**Q5.** A law enforcement agency in Germany wants to deploy real-time remote biometric identification (RBI) in a public square to search for missing children. What is the regulatory status under the EU AI Act?

A) Absolutely prohibited with no exceptions under Article 5
B) Permitted as an exception for search of missing children, subject to prior judicial authorization
C) Allowed as a high-risk AI system with conformity assessment by August 2026
D) Permitted only if the system includes human oversight and transparency disclosures

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: B**

While real-time RBI in public spaces is generally prohibited under Article 5, the EU AI Act includes specific exceptions for law enforcement, including the search for missing children, victims of trafficking, or abduction victims. These exceptions require judicial authorization.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- Real-time RBI is prohibited but includes specific law enforcement exceptions, so it is not absolutely prohibited.
- Real-time RBI in public spaces is addressed under prohibited practices (Article 5), not as a high-risk system requiring conformity assessment.
- The exception framework requires judicial authorization, not just human oversight and transparency measures.

**Q6.** A healthcare AI system classifies patients by analyzing their emotional state through facial recognition in a hospital emergency room to prioritize treatment. How is this system classified under the EU AI Act?

A) Minimal-risk AI with no specific obligations
B) Limited-risk AI requiring transparency disclosures only
C) High-risk AI under Annex III critical infrastructure category
D) Prohibited AI under Article 5 emotion recognition restrictions

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: C**

AI systems used in critical infrastructure for health (emergency room patient prioritization) are classified as high-risk under Annex III. While Article 5 prohibits emotion recognition in workplace and education, it does not prohibit it in healthcare settings when used for critical infrastructure purposes.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- Healthcare AI for patient prioritization is explicitly high-risk under critical infrastructure, not minimal-risk.
- This is a high-risk system requiring full compliance (risk management, documentation, etc.), not just transparency disclosures.
- Article 5's emotion recognition prohibition applies to workplace and education contexts, not healthcare critical infrastructure. The critical infrastructure classification takes precedence.

**Q7.** A company develops a general-purpose AI foundation model that is made available to the public. The model does not pose systemic risk. What obligations apply under the EU AI Act?

A) No obligations as general-purpose AI is outside the scope of the EU AI Act
B) Only prohibited practices compliance under Article 5
C) GPAI obligations under Articles 52-53, including technical documentation and transparency
D) Full high-risk AI requirements including conformity assessment and CE marking

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: C**

General-purpose AI (GPAI) models, including foundation models, have specific obligations under Articles 52-53. Even models without systemic risk must meet requirements like technical documentation and transparency, though the obligations are lighter than for systemic-risk GPAI.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- The EU AI Act explicitly covers general-purpose AI with dedicated provisions in Articles 52-53.
- GPAI has its own set of obligations beyond just prohibited practices compliance.
- GPAI obligations are distinct from high-risk AI requirements. Foundation models follow the GPAI framework, not high-risk AI conformity assessment unless deployed in high-risk use cases.

</details>

**Q8.** An employer in Spain implements an AI system to monitor employee productivity and uses emotion recognition technology to assess workplace satisfaction. The system is deployed in January 2026. What is the compliance status?

A) Compliant as a high-risk AI system if conformity assessment is completed by August 2026
B) Prohibited under Article 5 effective February 2, 2025 as emotion recognition in workplace
C) Compliant as limited-risk AI if employees are notified of the monitoring
D) Prohibited only if the system makes automated dismissal decisions without human review

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: B**

Article 5 explicitly prohibits emotion recognition AI systems in workplace and education settings. This prohibition became effective February 2, 2025, making the system illegal regardless of when it was deployed or what safeguards are in place.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- Emotion recognition in the workplace is prohibited, not classified as high-risk. No conformity assessment can make it compliant.
- Employee notification and transparency measures do not override the categorical prohibition on emotion recognition in workplace contexts.
- The prohibition applies to emotion recognition in the workplace generally, not just to specific automated decision-making scenarios like dismissals.

</details>
